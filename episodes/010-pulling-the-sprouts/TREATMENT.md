# Fable 10 — Pulling the Sprouts

**Source:** the impatient farmer of Song (Mencius 2A:2, public domain), moved to an old Japanese rice village and retold in original words.
**Logline:** A farmer cannot bear how slowly his rice grows, so one night he pulls every sprout a little higher. By sunrise the whole field is dead.
**Moral:** You cannot hurry what must grow.

## Three structures, and the choice
1. *Linear day → night → morning → rain* (one paddy, four impressions of the same blocks). The audience sees the same rows change four times.
2. *Start on the withered field*, flash back to the night. Stronger hook, but the song would have to tell the story backwards.
3. *The son's view only* (he wakes, runs, finds). Loses the pulling, which is the picture everyone remembers.
**Chosen: 1.** The fable is about time, so the print itself goes through time: one block set, re-inked for dawn, moonlight, sunrise and rain.

**Native move (emotional peak):** the wilt happens *inside one printed sheet* with no cut: the green block drains to straw while the key block folds, as if the print were being re-inked in front of us.

Scene changes sit in the held last note of the previous sung line so the scroll keeps about a second in song time.

## Sound
Song (Suno) carries voice and music. Underneath: spring ambience / night / spring / rain; wading splashes (L01–L02), frog croaks and drips while he pulls, running steps, one bell on the withered field, a soft thud when he bows, rain drips.
