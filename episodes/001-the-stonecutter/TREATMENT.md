# The Stonecutter — Treatment

Style: Ukiyo-e (`styles/ukiyoe/STYLE.md`). 1080×1920 (9:16), 24 fps, ~60 s, English voice + burned-in cartouche subtitles + .srt.
Source: Japanese folk tale "The Stonecutter" (public domain; retold in our own words).
Pilot for a moral-stories channel: every episode = one story, one wish/flaw, one turn, the moral printed as the final cartouche.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The wheel of wishes (chosen).** One constant subject — the mountain — appears in every print. Each wish is a new print sliding in on the handscroll; the stonecutter's form is printed block by block into each new identity. The last wish (the mountain) is chipped by a tiny stonecutter, and the wheel closes on the first print, reprinted in warm colours. Why: the folk tale *is* a cycle, and "one constant subject placed differently" is native to the medium.
2. **Before / after.** Two prints only, the same key block: grey dawn and golden dusk, the whole odyssey told as a flashback in the middle. Rejected: too static for a 60 s short; the transformations are the fun.
3. **The ladder.** A tall pillar print (hashira-e) the camera climbs: stonecutter at the bottom, lord, cloud, sun at the top — then falls back down. Rejected: in the tale the cloud beats the sun and the mountain beats the cloud, so a vertical hierarchy contradicts the story; and it gives away the ending.

Opening image (from the topic, not the style cliché): an extreme close crop of a chisel on grey stone, a spark — the sound comes first.
Ending: the opening print again, alternate impression (morning grey → evening persimmon), the man smiling; the print washes back to paper and only the red seal + moral remain.

## 2. Logline and arc

A poor stonecutter wishes to be anything mightier than himself — and discovers the mightiest thing at the foot of the mountain was him.

- Setup (0–6 s): he cuts stone, envies a passing lord.
- Turn chain (6–40 s): lord → sun → cloud → mountain; each is beaten by the next.
- Turn (40–47 s): the mountain feels a chisel. Silence. A stonecutter.
- Ending (47–60 s): he wishes to be himself; same print, warm; the moral.

## 3. Benchmark

- Learn from: Hiroshige's *Fifty-three Stations* (small figures, stacked mist bands, the journey as a series), Hokusai's *Thirty-six Views* (one constant mountain placed differently each time), the anime *Mononoke* (2007) (flat layers sliding, seals as screen language).
- Don't take: any specific print, the Great Wave or Red Fuji compositions, Mononoke's characters or designs, any melody.

## 6. Cue map

Free-rhythm shakuhachi (synthesised breath tone, miyako-bushi / in scale on D: D Eb G A Bb) against a shamisen pulse at 84 bpm.

| Section | Time | Music |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 | silence → chisel taps (wood/stone foley), a single shamisen sawari note on the title |
| Quarry | 3–9 | shamisen ostinato starts, hyōshigi on the cut |
| Lord / Sun | 9–23 | ostinato + shakuhachi phrases, taiko heartbeat under the sun |
| Cloud / Flood | 23–37 | koto glissando at the cloud; taiko tightens into a roll at the flood |
| Mountain | 37–43 | **silence** (ma); then tap… tap… |
| Blank | 43–47 | near-silence, a small bell |
| Return | 47–60 | shakuhachi alone, warm; accelerating hyōshigi + seal thump at the moral; ring out |

Every slide lands on a hyōshigi clap; every seal stamp has a thump.

## 8. Subtitles and titles

Subtitles = horizontal cream cartouche (thick + thin sumi border, small red seal at left), Shippori Mincho 500, bottom of frame; hold ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Each print carries a vertical view cartouche with the view title ("The Quarry", "The Lord", "The Sun", "The Cloud", "The Mountain", "The Stonecutter") in a brush font (Yuji Syuku). Moral = a large central cartouche on a blank sheet, stamped with a seal.
