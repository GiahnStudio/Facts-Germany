# 009 — Empty Your Cup (Zen teaching story, retold)

**Logline.** A scholar who has read everything climbs to an old master to "learn Zen" and never stops talking. The master pours his tea — and keeps pouring. Only an empty cup can be filled.

## Three structures considered
1. **Journey** (road → gate → room → road home): too close to 008's single road.
2. **One room, four seasons of light**: calm, but the overflow would stay small in the frame.
3. **Words as tea** (chosen): what fills the room (the scholar's words, printed as kanji that pile up in the air) is mirrored by what fills the cup. Close print of the cup at the centre, the room before and after as alternate impressions (day / dusk).

## Native move (emotional peak)
**The frame is an object.** The tea does not stop at the rim, nor at the table: it runs left, over the double ink border and into the mount of the scroll, and drips there. The camera follows it out of the print; the next print arrives from the direction the tea ran.
Second, quieter move: **alternate impression** — the same room reprinted at dusk; the words that filled it drop to the floor and the room is empty when he bows.
