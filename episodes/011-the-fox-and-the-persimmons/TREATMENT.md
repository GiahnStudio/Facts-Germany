# 011 — The Fox and the Persimmons

**Logline:** A hungry fox leaps all afternoon for persimmons he cannot reach, tumbles off a stone, declares them sour and stalks off — while a crow up in the branch eats one under the moon.
**Moral:** It's easy to scorn what you cannot reach. (small: The fruit was sweet all along.)
**Source:** Aesop (fox and grapes), moved to an autumn hill in old Japan; own words.

## Three structures considered
1. **One tree, one day** (chosen): the same key block printed three times — noon, sunset, dusk — so only the light and the fox change; the tree and its fruit never move. A fourth, new sheet answers the fox's lie.
2. Journey scroll: the fox passes three different trees. Rejected — dilutes the single unreachable thing.
3. The crow as narrator from the start. Rejected — gives the punchline away.

## Native move (emotional peak)
After three impressions of the wide landscape the scroll lands on a **bold close crop**: a huge paper-white moon, one diagonal branch, the crow biting into the fruit — and the fox reduced to a tiny orange figure on the ridge, nose still in the air. The irony is carried by scale alone.

## Sound
Ambience autumn wind → dusk. Paw steps, leap/landing thuds, scrape on the stone, big thud at the tumble, small bell at "sour", two caws, three pecks. Song carries voice and music.
